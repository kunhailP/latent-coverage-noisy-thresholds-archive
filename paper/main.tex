\title{Latent coverage of noisy calibration thresholds}

\author{KUN WOO PARK}
\affil{[Department, institution, postal address, country] \email{pkw31386094@gmail.com}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Prediction intervals for a latent quantity, such as the mean of an unsampled area, are often calibrated on noisy proxies with Gaussian errors. The noisy threshold is conservative for symmetric unimodal latent distributions, but symmetry is seldom plausible. For bi-log-concave latent distributions, which include the log-concave ones and some multimodal ones, we show that little is lost without it: at every noise level, noisy coverage 0.9 guarantees latent coverage 0.8991, against 0.8 from a shape-free transfer, and 0.8992 cannot be guaranteed, so a slack of one part in a thousand restores the nominal level. Hence the noisy conformal threshold at a suitable rank, at most three above the usual high-probability rank for 29 to 3000 calibration units, covers the latent target with high probability even when the noise variances are heterogeneous and unknown, whereas without the slack the latent reliability of the usual rank can tend to zero as the calibration sample grows. Behind this is a precise account of the worst-case widening of the calibrated radius, which is governed by the location of the latent mean. With the mean at a fixed distance from the boundary of the interval the widening is of the order of the noise variance, an order attained by asymmetric distributions with mean zero; it reaches the order of the noise standard deviation only when the mean lies within a bounded number of noise standard deviations of the boundary. Under log-concavity, known variances and a numerically computed radius shorten the interval by about ten per cent in simulations.
\end{abstract}

\begin{keywords}
Conformal prediction; Deconvolution; Log-concavity; Measurement error; Small area estimation.
\end{keywords}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Suppose that a residual $W$, the latent target minus a centre fitted on independent training data, is observed only through $V = W + e$, where $e \sim N(0, D)$ is independent of $W$ and, until \S~\ref{sec:hetero}, $D$ is known. A survey statistician who calibrates a prediction interval for the true mean of an unsampled area on the direct estimates of sampled areas is in this position \citep{fay1979}, as is a conformal analyst whose calibration responses carry measurement error \citep{einbinder2024, cohen2025}. The threshold $t$ at which the noisy scores $|V|$ have coverage $p$ is observable. We ask which radius gives the latent residual coverage $q$, uniformly over a class of latent distributions.

If $W$ is symmetric and unimodal, the theorem of \citet{anderson1955} gives $\pr(|W + e| \le t) \le \pr(|W| \le t)$, so the noisy threshold is conservative \citep{einbinder2024}. Without symmetry the available transfers make no shape assumption: a median-zero noise turns a noisy level $1 - \alpha$ into a latent level $1 - 2\alpha$ \citep{guille2024}, and LatentCP \citep{zheng2026}, with a marginal guarantee and no shape assumption, uses a Markov-type compatibility threshold. \citet{cohen2025} estimate the noise-free threshold by deconvolution, without a latent guarantee; conformal intervals for small areas \citep{bersson2024} cover observable responses rather than latent area means, and conformal confidence intervals \citep{zhang2026} target the expectations of sampled units rather than a new, unsampled one; \citet{armstrong2022} and \citet{ignatiadis2022} give robust and nonparametric empirical Bayes intervals for sampled units, and parametric small-area prediction intervals \citep{hallmaiti2006, yoshimori2014} rely on a model for the latent effects. For a fixed smooth latent distribution, Gaussian noise moves quantiles by $O(D)$ \citep{jochmans2024}; a guarantee, however, must hold for the worst distribution in a class, and there the order and its cause are different.

Uniformly over a shape class the picture is organized by the location of the mean. We work with log-concave laws and with the larger class of bi-log-concave laws \citep{dumbgen2017}, which does not require unimodality. Without a restriction on the mean the worst-case widening at equal levels is at most $c_q D^{1/2}$, with a constant that is sharp as $D \to 0$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:small}); if the mean lies at distance at least $d$ from the boundary of the interval the widening is at most $D/(2d)$ for every $D$, an order attained by asymmetric laws with mean zero (Theorem~\ref{thm:mean} and Supplementary Proposition~S1); and a widening of order $D^{1/2}$ requires the mean within $O(D^{1/2})$ of the boundary, with a limiting coefficient given by a one-dimensional extremum (Theorem~\ref{thm:transition}). The widening is also small, at most $1.16\%$ of the radius at $q = 0.9$. Measured in coverage, noisy coverage $0.9$ implies latent coverage $0.8991$ at every noise level, where the shape-free transfer of \citet{guille2024} gives $0.8$, and $0.8992$ cannot be guaranteed (Corollary~\ref{cor:slack}). This is what makes the precise worst case useful: a slack of one part in a thousand absorbs the widening for every noise variance at once, so the noisy threshold at a suitable rank is valid for the latent target when the noise variances are heterogeneous and unknown (Proposition~\ref{prop:unknown}), and knowledge of the variances is needed only for efficiency (\S~\ref{sec:hetero}). Without the slack the guarantee is not merely slightly off: for some log-concave laws the latent reliability of the usual high-probability rank tends to zero as the calibration sample grows (Proposition~\ref{prop:fail}), which matters for conformal calibration on large sets of noisy regression responses more than for small areas.

\section{The sharp radius}\label{sec:setup}

Let $\LC$ be the class of log-concave laws on the real line, including point masses, and $\mathcal B \supset \LC$ the class of laws that are point masses or have a continuous distribution function $F$ with $\log F$ and $\log(1 - F)$ concave. Both classes are closed under translation and scaling, and convolution with a log-concave law preserves them \citep{prekopa1973, saumard2019}. After scaling $t$ to $1$, write $x = D/t^2$ and let $Z \sim N(0,1)$ be independent of $W$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:R}
R_{p,q}(x) = \sup\{ Q_q(|W|) : W \in \LC,\ \pr(|W + x^{1/2}Z| \le 1) \ge p \},
\end{equation}
where $Q_q$ is the lower $q$-quantile, and $R_{p,q}(x) = -\infty$ if nothing is feasible, which happens exactly when $\pr(|x^{1/2}Z| \le 1) < p$. If calibration certifies noisy coverage at least $p$ at threshold $t$, then $t\,R_{p,q}(D/t^2)$ is the smallest radius that guarantees latent coverage $q$ for every log-concave latent law; this is sharpness within the class and for one constraint, not optimality among procedures that use the whole calibration sample. We call $R_{p,q}(x) - 1$ the radius correction. Let $R^{\mathcal B}_{p,q}$ be defined by \eqref{eq:R} with $\mathcal B$ in place of $\LC$, so $R^{\mathcal B}_{p,q} \ge R_{p,q}$. The upper bounds of \S\S~\ref{sec:mean}--\ref{sec:slack} hold for $R^{\mathcal B}_{p,q}$; the exact radius $R_{p,q}$ at a fixed $x$ and its computation use log-concavity. For $0 \le b \le 1$ let $R^{(b)}_{p,q}(x)$ be defined by \eqref{eq:R} with the additional constraint $|E(W)| \le b$, so that $1 - b$ bounds the distance of the mean from the boundary.

Write $g_x(w) = \pr(|w + x^{1/2}Z| \le 1)$, so that the constraint in \eqref{eq:R} reads $E\{g_x(W)\} \ge p$, and let $\mathcal F$ be the family of point masses and of laws with density proportional to $e^{\beta w}$ on a segment $[a,b]$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:reduction}
$R_{p,q}(x) = \sup\{Q_q(|W|) : W \in \mathcal F,\ E\{g_x(W)\} \ge p\}$. The same holds for any continuous constraint kernel that vanishes at $\pm\infty$, in particular for the average kernel of \S~\ref{sec:hetero}.
\end{proposition}

The proof uses the localization theorem of \citet{fradelizi2004} (Supplementary Material); over $\mathcal F$ the problem is a two-dimensional maximization of closed forms.

\section{Worst-case radius correction and the location of the mean}\label{sec:mean}

\subsection{A one-sided problem}

Near each end of the interval the problem is one-sided. For $u > 0$ let $E_u$ be exponential with rate $u$, independent of $Z$, and for the exponential tail $b - E_u$ let
\[
m_u(b) = \pr(b - E_u + Z \le 0) = \Phi(-b) + e^{-ub + u^2/2}\,\Phi(b - u),
\]
which is continuous and strictly decreasing in $b$. Let $b_u(p)$ solve $m_u(b) = p$, and write $v_q(u) = b_u(q) - u^{-1}\log(1/q)$ and $k_q(u) = u^{-1} - b_u(q)$. The law $b_u(q) - E_u$ has $q$-quantile $v_q(u)$ and mean $-k_q(u)$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:onesided}
Let $c_{p,q} = \sup\{F_Y^{-1}(q) : Y \in \LC,\ \pr(Y + Z \le 0) \ge p\}$ and $c_q = c_{q,q}$. Then $c_{p,q} = \sup_{u > 0}\{b_u(p) - u^{-1}\log(1/q)\}$, $c_q > 0$, and $q \mapsto c_q$ is nonincreasing. The same holds with $\mathcal B$ in place of $\LC$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:meanonesided}
Let $e^{-1} < q < 1$ and $\kappa \ge 0$. Then
\[
\sup\{F_Y^{-1}(q) : Y \in \mathcal B,\ E(Y) \le -\kappa,\ \pr(Y + Z \le 0) \ge q\} = L_q(\kappa) = \sup\{v_q(u) : u > 0,\ k_q(u) \ge \kappa\}.
\]
The function $L_q$ is positive and nonincreasing, and, for $q > 1/2$, $L_q(\kappa) = c_q$ for $\kappa \le \kappa^*_q$, where $\kappa^*_q$ is the largest value of $k_q$ on the compact set of maximizers of $v_q$, and $\kappa L_q(\kappa) \to \{1 - \log(1/q)\}/2$ as $\kappa \to \infty$.
\end{lemma}

Exponential tails are extremal because concavity of $\log F_Y$ limits how fast mass can accumulate below the $q$-quantile; the proofs replace $Y$ by the exponential tail through a supergradient of $\log F_Y$ at that quantile, which keeps the quantile, the constraint and the bound on the mean. Ball arithmetic gives $c_{0.8} = 0.04607$, $c_{0.9} = 0.01906$ and $c_{0.95} = 0.00841$, and in double precision $\kappa^*_q = 6.81$, $20.18$ and $50.07$.

\subsection{No restriction on the mean}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:small}
For every $q \in (0,1)$, every $x > 0$ and every $W \in \mathcal B$ with $\pr(|W + x^{1/2}Z| \le 1) \ge q$, $Q_q(|W|) \le 1 + c_q x^{1/2}$. Moreover $R_{q,q}(x) = 1 + c_q x^{1/2} + o(x^{1/2})$ as $x \to 0$, and likewise for $R^{\mathcal B}_{q,q}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[of the upper bound]
Let $\alpha_R = \pr(W + x^{1/2}Z > 1)$ and $\alpha_L = \pr(W + x^{1/2}Z < -1)$, so that $\alpha_L + \alpha_R \le 1 - q$. The law of $Y = (W - 1)/x^{1/2}$ has $\log F_Y$ concave and $\pr(Y + Z \le 0) = 1 - \alpha_R$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:onesided}, $F_Y^{-1}(1 - \alpha_R) \le c_{1-\alpha_R} \le c_q$, that is, $\pr(W > 1 + c_q x^{1/2}) \le \alpha_R$. The same argument applied to $-W$, which uses concavity of $\log(1 - F_W)$, gives $\pr(W < -1 - c_q x^{1/2}) \le \alpha_L$, so $\pr(|W| \le 1 + c_q x^{1/2}) \ge q$.
\end{proof}

The lower bound uses boundary-layer laws $W = 1 + x^{1/2}\{b_u(q + \eta_x) - E_u\}$, with a cut-off just outside the threshold (Supplementary Material). Since $E\{g_x(W)\} \le g_x(0)$, the constraint is feasible only if $x^{1/2} \le 1/\Phi^{-1}\{(1+q)/2\}$, so Theorem~\ref{thm:small} gives $Q_q(|W|) - 1 \le c_q/\Phi^{-1}\{(1+q)/2\}$, which is below $1.16\%$ at $q = 0.9$ for every $W \in \mathcal B$. For log-concave laws, double-precision upper bounds give $R_{q,q}(x) \le 1$ for $x \ge 0.013$, hence a correction below $0.22\%$ (Supplementary Material).

\subsection{The mean away from the boundary}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:mean}
Let $W \in \mathcal B$ with $|E(W)| \le B$, let $\sigma > 0$ and $t > B$, and suppose that $\pr(|W + \sigma Z| \le t) \ge q$. Then
\[
Q_q(|W|) \le B + \{(t - B)^2 + \sigma^2\}^{1/2} \le t + \frac{\sigma^2}{2(t - B)}.
\]
In particular $R^{(b)}_{p,q}(x) \le 1 + x/\{2(1 - b)\}$ for all $p \ge q$ and $x > 0$, and $Q_q(|W|) \le (t^2 + \sigma^2)^{1/2}$ if $E(W) = 0$.
\end{theorem}

A widening needs a density that rises steeply towards an end of the interval, and a mean far from that end caps the rate of rise. The proof in the Supplementary Material makes this precise with an exponential-tail comparison and the heat equation for quantiles \citep{steinbrecher2008}, which give $d\{r(s) - B\}^2/ds \ge -1$ for $r(s) = Q_q(|W + s^{1/2}Z|)$. It improves on Theorem~\ref{thm:small} only when $x^{1/2} < 2c_q(1 - b)/(1 - c_q^2)$; for mean-zero laws at $q = 0.9$ this means $x < 1.5\times10^{-3}$, where both bounds are below $0.08\%$. The order $x$ cannot be improved, even for mean-zero laws: centred log-affine laws on a segment attain it, with coefficient up to $0.0747$ at $q = 0.9$ (Supplementary Proposition~S1), so centring does not replace symmetry.

\subsection{The transition}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:transition}
Let $1 - e^{-1} < q < 1$, $x > 0$ and $W \in \mathcal B$ with $\pr(|W + x^{1/2}Z| \le 1) \ge q$. Then $|E(W)| < 1$, and if $\Delta = Q_q(|W|) - 1 > 0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:necessary}
2\{1 - |E(W)|\}\Delta + \Delta^2 \le x .
\end{equation}
Moreover, for every $\kappa \ge 0$,
\[
\lim_{x \to 0} x^{-1/2}\{R^{(b_x)}_{q,q}(x) - 1\} = L_q(\kappa), \qquad b_x = 1 - \kappa x^{1/2},
\]
and the same limit holds with $\LC$ replaced by $\mathcal B$ in the definition of $R^{(b)}$.
\end{theorem}

In the original units the mean lies $\kappa D^{1/2}$ from the threshold, and the unrestricted worst case $c_q D^{1/2}$ persists until $\kappa$ exceeds $\kappa^*_q$, about $20$ noise standard deviations at $q = 0.9$; beyond it the coefficient decays like $\{1 - \log(1/q)\}/(2\kappa)$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:transition}). Both parts are proved in the Supplementary Material.

\section{Calibration slack and a finite-sample procedure}\label{sec:procedure}

\subsection{Slack removes the correction}\label{sec:slack}

The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:small} splits the noisy failure budget between the two ends. With a noisy level $p \ge q$, splitting both the noisy and the latent budgets gives, for every $x$ and every $W \in \mathcal B$ feasible at level $p$, $Q_q(|W|) \le 1 + C_{p,q}x^{1/2}$, where $C_{p,q} \ge c_{p,q}$, nonincreasing in $p$, is a worst case over splits of the one-sided constants, defined in the Supplementary Material.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:slack}
If $C_{p,q} \le 0$ then $R^{\mathcal B}_{p,q}(x) \le 1$ for every $x$, and if $c_{p,q} > 0$ then $R_{p,q}(x) > 1$ for all small $x$. At $q = 0.9$ the smallest slack $p - q$ with $C_{p,q} \le 0$, and with $R_{p,q}(x) \le 1$ for all $x$, lies in $(7.9\times10^{-4}, 10^{-3}]$; other levels are in the Supplementary Material. In coverage terms, every $W \in \mathcal B$ with noisy coverage at least $0.8$, $0.9$ or $0.95$ has latent coverage at least $0.795$, $0.8991$ or $0.9498$, whatever the noise variance, while $0.7954$, $0.8992$ and $0.9499$ fail for some log-concave laws at small $x$.
\end{corollary}


\subsection{Heterogeneous and unknown noise variances}\label{sec:hetero}

Let $W_1, \ldots, W_K, W_{\rm new}$ be independent with law $G$, independent of $e_i \sim N(0, D_i)$ with the $D_i$ fixed. The scores $V_i = W_i + e_i$ are independent but not identically distributed, and $\mathcal D$ denotes the calibration data.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:beta}
Let $T = |V|_{(k)}$, let $\bar F$ be the average distribution function of the $|V_i|$, assumed continuous, and let $p_k$ be the $\delta$-quantile of the Beta$(k, K+1-k)$ distribution. If $k \ge Kp_k + 1$ then $\pr\{\bar F(T) \ge p_k\} \ge 1 - \delta$.
\end{proposition}

The proof uses \citet{hoeffding1956}; see also \citet{sen1970}.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:unknown}
Let $G \in \mathcal B$, let the $D_i$ be unknown, and suppose that $k \ge Kp_k + 1$ and $C_{p_k,q} \le 0$. Then $\pr_{\mathcal D}\{\pr(|W_{\rm new}| \le T \mid \mathcal D) \ge q\} \ge 1 - \delta$.
\end{proposition}


\begin{proof}
On the event of Proposition~\ref{prop:beta}, $K^{-1}\sum_i \pr(|W + e_i| \le T) \ge p_k$ for $W \sim G$. If $\pr(|W| \le T) < q$, Corollary~\ref{cor:slack} at threshold $T$ gives $\pr(|W + e_i| \le T) < p_k$ for every $i$, whatever $D_i$, and so for the average, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

At $q = 0.9$ and $\delta = 0.05$ the conditions hold at the smallest rank with $p_k \ge 0.901$ and $k \ge Kp_k + 1$, which exists for $K \ge 29$ and, for $K \le 3000$, is at most three ranks above the usual high-probability rank, the smallest with $p_k \ge 0.9$ (Supplementary Material). For $K = 110$ the two coincide, at $k = 105$ with $p_k = 0.9068$. The marginal rank $\lceil 0.9(K+1)\rceil = 100$ is not covered, and for large $K$ neither the marginal nor the usual rank is valid for the latent target in general (Proposition~\ref{prop:fail}).

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:fail}
Let $q \in (0,1)$. There are a log-concave law $G$ and $D > 0$ such that, with $D_i = D$ for all $i$, $\pr_{\mathcal D}\{\pr(|W_{\rm new}| \le |V|_{(k_K)} \mid \mathcal D) \ge q\} \to 0$ as $K \to \infty$ for every sequence of ranks with $k_K/K \to q$, such as the marginal rank and, for any fixed $\delta$, the usual high-probability rank.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since $c_q > 0$, Theorem~\ref{thm:small} gives $R_{q,q}(x) > 1$ for some $x$, so some log-concave $W$ has $H(1) \ge q$ and $r_q = Q_q(|W|) > 1$, where $H$ is the distribution function of $|W + x^{1/2}Z|$; take $G$ the law of $W$ and $D = x$. Latent coverage at $T = |V|_{(k)}$ is at least $q$ if and only if $T \ge r_q$, that is, if and only if fewer than $k$ of the $|V_i|$ are below $r_q$, a ${\rm Bin}\{K, H(r_q)\}$ count. As $H$ is strictly increasing on $(0, \infty)$, $H(r_q) > H(1) \ge q$, so $\pr\{{\rm Bin}(K, H(r_q)) \le k_K - 1\} \to 0$ by the law of large numbers.
\end{proof}

The proof also gives the exact latent reliability of rank $k$ with $D_i = D$, $\pr\{{\rm Bin}(K, H(r_q)) \le k - 1\}$. At the boundary-layer law of the Supplementary Material, with $D^{1/2}$ equal to $1\%$ of the noisy $q$-quantile, $H(r_q) = 0.9008$, and the usual rank has latent reliability $0.943$, $0.918$, $0.790$ and $0.151$ at $K = 10^3$, $10^4$, $10^5$ and $10^6$, against $0.954$, $0.958$, $0.968$ and $0.989$ for the certified rank. For $29 \le K \le 300$, as in small areas, the usual rank falls at most $0.005$ below the target at this law, with $0.967$ at $K = 110$; with $K$ in the hundreds of thousands its reliability collapses, although its latent coverage tends to $0.89918$, close to $0.9$. Known variances shorten the threshold.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:simple}
Let $G \in \mathcal B$ and $k \ge Kp_k + 1$. If $D_i \ge D_{\min}$ for all $i$ and $C_{p',q} < 0$ for some $p'$ with $q \le p' \le p_k$, then $s = \max\{0, T + C_{p',q}D_{\min}^{1/2}\}$ satisfies $\pr_{\mathcal D}\{\pr(|W_{\rm new}| \le s \mid \mathcal D) \ge q\} \ge 1 - \delta$, whether or not the $D_i$ are otherwise known.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
On the event of Proposition~\ref{prop:beta}, some $i$ has $\pr(|W + e_i| \le T) \ge p_k \ge p'$, and the bound of \S~\ref{sec:slack} at $x = D_i/T^2$ gives $Q_q(|W|) \le T + C_{p',q}D_i^{1/2} \le T + C_{p',q}D_{\min}^{1/2}$.
\end{proof}

At $q = 0.9$, $K = 110$ and $\delta = 0.05$, where $p_k = 0.906806$, ball arithmetic gives $C_{0.9068,0.9} \le -0.114$, hence $s = T - 0.114 D_{\min}^{1/2}$; plugging in the smallest variance instead is not valid (Supplementary Material). The sharpest use of known variances is the average kernel $\bar g(w) = K^{-1}\sum_i \pr(|w + e_i| \le T)$: $\int \bar g\, dG \ge p_k$ on the event of Proposition~\ref{prop:beta}, so by Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction} the computed radius $s = T R^{\rm mix}_{p_k - \varepsilon, q}(D_1/T^2, \ldots, D_K/T^2)$, with $\varepsilon$ bounding a discretization error, has the same guarantee for log-concave $G$.

For the computed radius our implementation returns a numerical upper bound on $R^{\rm mix}$, by a branch and bound in double precision, not a certificate.

\section{Numerical illustration}\label{sec:numerical}

The target is $\pr_{\mathcal D}\{\pr(W_{\rm new} \in C \mid \mathcal D) \ge 0.9\} \ge 0.95$, with $K = 110$, $\var(W) = 1$ and heterogeneous noise variances $D_i = 0.577\,L_i/E(L_i)$, $L_i$ lognormal with log-scale $0.7$. Latent residuals, all with mean zero, are drawn directly; conditional coverage given each data set is exact, and reliability is estimated from $150$ data sets per law. Besides four log-concave laws we use a truncated exponential law in $\mathcal F$, a bimodal law in $\mathcal B$ but not in $\LC$, and a $t_3$ law outside $\mathcal B$. We compare the three rules of \S~\ref{sec:hetero}, a Fay--Herriot tolerance interval adapted from \citet{chatterjee2008}, and the Markov rule applied to $\bar g_T(0) - \bar g_T(W) \ge 0$, the smallest radius the single constraint allows without a shape assumption, at its best rank, $107$ (Supplementary Material).

The rules with a guarantee reached the target for every law in their class, and the computed radius did so also under the bimodal and $t_3$ laws, for which it has none. Corollary~\ref{cor:simple} was $1.2$--$1.4\%$ shorter than the noisy threshold, and the computed radius $9.5$--$12\%$ shorter than the noisy threshold and $25$--$28\%$ shorter than the Markov rule. The Fay--Herriot interval was $7$--$15\%$ shorter than the computed radius but missed the target under the truncated Laplace law by $5.8$ standard errors; its other shortfalls are within Monte Carlo error. With $2000$ data sets per law the noisy threshold, Corollary~\ref{cor:simple} and the Markov rule reached conditional coverage $0.9$ in every data set, with mean conditional coverage $0.97$--$0.99$ for the first two, so the guarantees are conservative for laws far from the extremal ones of \S~\ref{sec:mean}. The Supplementary Material compares LatentCP \citep{zheng2026}, $16$--$18\%$ longer than the computed radius, and the deconvolution threshold of \citet{cohen2025}, which has no latent guarantee, and shows that at an oracle-matched reliability the gains lie in the cost of a guarantee rather than in the shape of the interval.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

The guarantees are conditional on the calibration sample and assume independent Gaussian noise and a bi-log-concave, or for the computed radius log-concave, latent law shared by the calibration areas and the new one. The target is an unsampled area; for a sampled area the direct estimate is itself informative, and intervals for the expectations of sampled units are a different problem \citep{zhang2026}. The shared law is the substantive assumption. In the Supplementary Material, shifting the new residual by half a latent standard deviation lowered mean coverage by up to $0.027$ for laws like those of Table~\ref{tab:synth}, but near the extremal law a tenth of a standard deviation breaks the guarantee; and if residual dispersion varies with the noise variance, reliability averaged over new areas was kept in our designs but fell to $0.75$ given a new area's variance; no guarantee covers either case. Bi-log-concavity cannot be checked on the latent scale, but Gaussian convolution preserves it, so with equal variances a confidence band for the signed noisy residuals containing no bi-log-concave distribution function \citep{dumbgen2017} rules it out, though not conversely; it excludes heavy tails, although the symmetric $t_3$ law of Table~\ref{tab:synth} is covered by Anderson's theorem. The mean condition of Theorems~\ref{thm:mean} and~\ref{thm:transition} concerns the latent residual law, which an intercept in the fitted regression does not centre. Since $E(V) = E(W)$ the mean is identified, and a bound on $|E(W)|$ with Theorem~\ref{thm:mean} could correct the radius, but at $q = 0.9$ only where both bounds are below $0.08\%$ of it.

Open questions include non-Gaussian noise, the centred-law coefficient, in $[0.0747, 0.5]$ at $q = 0.9$, a median version of Theorem~\ref{thm:mean}, and whether $C_{p,q} = c_{p,q}$ and $R^{\mathcal B}_{p,q} = R_{p,q}$.
